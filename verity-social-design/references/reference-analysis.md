# Reference analysis: B2B SaaS and operations Meta creatives

Source: the Meta Ad Library (public), pulled 2026-10-02 by advertiser page. 55 image creatives were studied as contact
sheets:

- Intercom (Fin), 9
- Ramp, 12
- Deel, 10
- Toast, 12 (restaurant POS, the closest category to Verity's Food & Hospitality)
- Notion, 12

These are design education only. We take principles, never branding, layouts or copy.

## What each one teaches

**Intercom (Fin): the closest to the Verity world**
- A dark atmospheric field, a light product UI card, and a light-weight editorial headline. That is very nearly Verity's
  locked system, which proves it works as a paid format.
- The headline sits top-left in 3-4 lines. The brand mark sits top-right on the headline's cap line. Furniture never
  moves across the set.
- The UI card is anchored to the bottom-right and bleeds off the bottom edge. Its left edge sits on a column line, not
  centred.
- The CTA is micro text, uppercase, underlined and placed directly under the headline ("SEE FIN IN ACTION"). It is
  readable without shouting.
- Real product content in the UI (a chat with a real-looking order number, an ROI calculator with numbers) makes the
  idea legible without a caption.
- Weakness: some headlines run 4 lines of mixed sizes, and the aurora background edges toward "AI glow".

**Ramp: hierarchy and restraint in paid**
- A two-zone split: the hero object (the card) fills the top half, and copy plus CTA fill the bottom on a flat field.
  Each zone has one job.
- Data-led creatives work: one huge number ("$0k") at about 4x the headline, light weight.
- One accent (acid yellow) is reserved for the CTA or the one key number. It is never decorative.
- The product UI card ("Banking + Treasury, balance $751k") is cropped and shown large on a dark field. Real numbers, no
  feature list.
- Weakness: the cash-back variants carry 3 lines of body copy, which is too much for a thumb-stop.

**Deel: workflow and callouts**
- The workflow ad ("Source, match, and hire candidates") shows 3 UI chips joined by a dashed path. It is a process
  diagram made from real UI pieces, which proves archetype E.
- Callout chips attach to the photo or UI edge they describe ("96% match" sits on the portrait's edge). They never float.
- The brand bar is fixed: logo bottom-left, tagline next to it, arrow bottom-right. It is identical across the set, so the
  campaign reads as one family.
- Weakness: surreal photo collage (clouds, unicorns) is a strong look but not Verity's. Verity never uses it.

**Toast: industry-led**
- The product is shown in its real environment (a terminal on a restaurant counter, handhelds in a server's hand). The
  industry is legible before any word is read.
- The headline is about the operator's world ("Finally, a restaurant POS with no hidden fees", "Serve anywhere").
- Weakness: heavy bold, centred type and two-colour headlines. Verity's equivalent is light weight, left-aligned and
  two-tone ink and muted.

**Notion: what not to do**
- Centred stacks of logo, headline, subhead, a blue pill and an illustration. They are generic and interchangeable, and
  the checklist creative is a feature list.
- One good lesson: a parallel two-part line ("Organize everything. Accomplish anything.") reads instantly. Verity's
  two-tone statement does the same job with tone instead of bold.

## Principles extracted

| Area | Principle |
|---|---|
| Composition | Two zones with one job each (statement / product), or one statement with one anchored object. Never three equal things. |
| Typography | Light-weight editorial headlines, 2-4 lines, left-aligned, short words. One size step down for support, then micro. Bold and centred reads as consumer or template. |
| Grid | Fixed furniture across a campaign (logo corner, CTA position, page bar) creates family resemblance. |
| Spacing | Generous: the headline block rarely takes more than 30% of the height; the object gets the rest. |
| Negative space | Used on the flat field around the headline; the object is cropped, not floated in space. |
| Information density | One idea, one proof. The best ones have 7-15 words in total on the creative. |
| Hierarchy | Headline, then object, then CTA. A number replaces the headline in data-led creatives. |
| Image and UI integration | Real UI, cropped large, bleeding off an edge, with real-looking data. Callouts attach to elements. |
| Cropping | UI enters from an edge (bottom-right is most common), keeps its top-left chrome, and loses its trailing edge. |
| Visual rhythm (sets) | Same furniture, same type scale; change the object and the crop. |
| Carousel sequencing | Covers that feel incomplete; one point per slide; a continuous element across slides (see `carousel-system.md`). |
| CTA | Either a small underlined micro link under the headline (organic, editorial) or one solid pill in the accent (paid). Never both. |
| Ad readability | The idea reads from the headline plus the object alone. Body copy belongs in the primary text, not on the image. |

## Applied to Verity

Intercom's structure, Ramp's restraint and data discipline, Deel's workflow chips and fixed furniture, Toast's
industry-first framing. All of it is rendered in Verity's own world: dark field, light glass UI, Inter 300 two-tone
statements, and accent only for state and CTA.
